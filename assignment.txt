Background and Motivation

Traffic forecasting is an important application of spatio-temporal machine learning. Given historical
traffic measurements from sensors installed across a road network, the objective is to predict traffic
conditions at future timestamps.
Traditional time-series forecasting models, such as ARIMA, LSTM, and temporal convolutional
networks, primarily learn patterns along the temporal dimension. However, traffic conditions at
different locations are not independent. Congestion at one location may affect nearby road seg-
ments, and traffic conditions may propagate through connected regions.
To capture these dependencies, we can represent the traffic sensor network as a graph. A graph is
defined as
                                          G = (V, E),
where V represents the set of nodes and E represents the set of edges connecting those nodes. In
our traffic forecasting problem, each node represents a traffic sensor, and edges represent spatial
relationships between sensors.

How Graph Neural Networks Use Adjacency

A Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) updates the representation of each node by aggregating
information from its neighbors. A common GCN operation is
                                         (︂                          )︂
                              H (l+1) = σ D̃−1/2 ÃD̃−1/2 H (l) W (l) ,

where:

   • Ã = A + I includes self-loops;
   • D̃ is the diagonal degree matrix of Ã;
   • H (l) contains node features at layer l;
   • W (l) contains learnable weights;
   • σ is an activation function.

This expression applies directly to a symmetric, nonnegative adjacency matrix. Directed graphs
may require different normalization or message-passing conventions.
The key idea is that each node learns from its own features and those of its graph neighbors. In
traffic forecasting, this allows a sensor’s representation to incorporate traffic measurements from
other sensors connected through the road network.
